    The line at the click

    The guardrails stop at the click. How a module navigates inside itself is entirely the module team’s call, whether it’s a Compass page, an app, an IIS tool or a Secured Zone app.

    Yours

    Internal menus, tabs, filters, queues and the landing view, and every screen in your SSO app, IIS tool or Secured Zone app.

    Shared

    The entry in the Compass left menu: its type, group, name and what a click on it does.

    Where they meet

    A screen inside your module that needs its own left-menu entry must be a module in its own right (guardrail 3), and its title must match the entry’s name (guardrail 2).

    Tip. Building an SSO app, IIS tool or Secured Zone app? Design its menus however suits your users. Only its entry in the Compass left menu follows the guardrails.

    Five guardrails

    Every module follows these five. They’re built into the shared menu component and the , so nobody approves them module by module. Open one to see why it exists and what it means for your team.

    1A group never turns into one of its childrenChildren are always listed by their own names, even when there is only one.

    In full. A click opens a child only if the group’s owner has set a ; otherwise the group only expands.

    Why. Clients must see the name of the feature they’re in. Today, a user with only Form-based sees Onboarding turn into Form-based.

    For your team. If your module sits in a group, it always shows under its own name.

    See it in practiceTry it in the simulator
    2One name everywhereThe registered name is the menu label, the page title, and the name in alerts and System Health Events.

    In full. The name is registered once, in English and French. A child’s page title reads Group: Name. Nothing else retypes it.

    Why. Today Form-based onboarding has four names in four places, so an alert can’t be matched to the menu.

    For your team. Pick one name for your module at registration and use it everywhere.

    See it in practiceTry it in the registry
    3Only modules get an entry, two levels deepCapability first, module second. Functions and permissions live inside the module.

    In full. The first level is a capability, the second a module, and nothing nests deeper. A such as view, manage or approve never gets its own entry.

    Why. Permissions change what a user can do on a page, not where they go.

    For your team. A queue, tab or approval step is part of your page, not a menu entry.

    See it in practiceRun it through the three questions
    4The menu follows access, the same way every timeIt shows exactly what the user is entitled to, and the same user and firm always get the same menu.

    In full. Nothing assigned disappears without a reason on the user record, and nothing appears unexpectedly.

    Why. In one call, Radhika’s profile rendered three different menus.

    For your team. Declare any your module needs, such as a SecureZone ID.

    See it in practiceTry reloading in the simulator
    5Every entry is declared in the feature registryType, group, name, any default child and linked IDs are recorded before activation.

    In full. Nothing is hard-coded in the menu: change the record, and the menu, page title and alerts follow.

    Why. One change in one place keeps every surface in step.

    For your team. Fill in the registry entry at feature registration, as part of the .

              Who decides what

              Decisions sit with the people closest to them; the steps in only where the shared menu changes shape. Click a layer.

              When to ask the Platform PM. Only for a new first-level item, a move between groups, or an exception to a guardrail.

              Best practices

              Recommendations for capability and module PMs; each PM decides how to apply them.

              • Place a module where “Group: Child” reads true to a client.CRS-FATCA is filed after accounts are opened, so it belongs with Clients and Accounts, not Onboarding.
              • Give every external app a home under its capability.Tools isn’t a catch-all. An IIS tool for servicing documents belongs under Servicing.
              • Set a default child only when a group has a clear primary route.Onboarding opens Form-based. Servicing has its own page, so it has no default.
              • Name entries by what the client does, in business terms.No vendor or project names such as IIS, NEST or SOMA. A child doesn’t repeat its group: Uploads under Servicing, not Servicing: Uploads.
              • Put features about one client or account on the client and account pages.Statements, tax documents and trade confirmations open from the client’s page, not the left menu.
              • Keep separators between groups of entries, not around single items.A separator shows only with visible entries on both sides: never first, never last, never two in a row.

                              Three menus that show the guardrails at work, and the cases teams ask about most.

                              Onboarding: a group with a default set by its owner

                              Onboarding has no page of its own. Its children are Form-based and Paper upload, and its owner set Form-based as the default. CRS-FATCA moved to Clients and Accounts, because it’s filed after accounts are opened.

                              Servicing: a group with page and a new child from IIS

                              Servicing opens its service requests page; Approvals and the planned Uploads sit under it. Pick a user to see their Servicing entry, then click it.

                                Orders: the reference

                                With the Orders page, a click opens it and lists GIC. With GIC only, Orders just expands and lists GIC. Every other group should behave the same way. Try GIC only in the simulator.
